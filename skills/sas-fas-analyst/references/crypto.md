# Crypto analysis

## Route before calculating

Classify the asset as L1/L2, DeFi, DeAI/DePIN, stablecoin, meme, or hybrid. Identify the chain and canonical contract. Separate protocol usage, token demand, and holder value capture.

## Token supply pressure

Use dimensionally consistent ratios:

- unlock pressure = monthly newly liquid supply value ÷ trailing 30-day credible spot volume;
- daily sell pressure = expected daily sellable issuance value ÷ credible daily spot volume;
- gross annual inflation = annual new issuance ÷ current circulating supply;
- net dilution = issuance − provable burns or permanent removals.

Estimate recipient sell-through when evidence exists. Do not treat exchange-reported volume as fully credible without quality checks. Continuous miner, validator, team, treasury, or foundation emissions are supply pressure even when they are not called unlocks.

## Real demand and real yield

Separate:

1. external customer payments;
2. protocol fees or revenue;
3. token incentives and subsidies;
4. value actually reaching holders.

Negative real yield alone does not establish a Ponzi-like structure. Require the combination of subsidy-dependent retention, weak external demand, missing value capture, and new capital funding earlier rewards before using that label.

## Value-capture ladder

- Tier 0: enforceable stable-asset distribution or automatic revenue-funded buy and burn.
- Tier 1: automatic fee sharing or productive staking funded by external revenue.
- Tier 2: token is an indispensable productive collateral, resource-consumption, or routing asset.
- Tier 3: revenue enters a treasury and holder benefit depends on governance.
- Tier 4: governance or narrative only, with no demonstrated economic sink.

State the escape valves: forks, substitutes, fee switches, governance changes, wrapped versions, and off-token payment paths.

## Route-specific checks

- L1/L2: fees, economic throughput, security budget, validator concentration, sequencer or bridge risk, stablecoin and developer retention.
- DeFi: organic TVL, fee quality, recursive leverage, bad debt, oracle and admin-key risk, liquidity depth, and governance capture.
- DeAI/DePIN: external paying demand, useful work verification, hardware or inference economics, emissions dependence, and whether token price reflexively determines measured output.
- Stablecoin: reserve composition, attestations, redemption, banking concentration, legal claims, duration, and run liquidity.
- Meme: liquidity depth relative to market cap, holder concentration, distribution channels, contract controls, and reflexivity. Do not invent fundamental cash flows.

Use MVRV, TVL, active addresses, and wallet concentration only when data coverage and interpretation are valid. No universal MVRV threshold establishes a bottom.

## Governance and security

Inspect upgrade control, timelock, multisig threshold, privileged roles, treasury, emission switches, bridge dependencies, incident history, audits, bug bounties, and regulatory exposure by jurisdiction. A mutable protocol is not automatically unsafe; price the control and process risk.
